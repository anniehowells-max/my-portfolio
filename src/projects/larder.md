## Challenge

Most recipe apps fall into one of two camps: storage, where links get saved and never opened again, or feeds built for endless scrolling. Neither suits people who cook often and with intention. They adapt recipes, cook the same dish many times over and want to remember what worked.

Larder grew out of an earlier shared recipe web app into a standalone iOS product. The challenge was to design and ship a complete app as a solo designer and developer: an identity that sits closer to independent food writing than to tech, an experience that makes saving and returning to recipes feel calm, and the infrastructure behind accounts, subscriptions and a social layer, all built to App Store standard.

||

## Solution

I took Larder from concept to launch, covering brand, product design and engineering. The identity draws on editorial food media, with serif display type, a restrained palette and a plain, warm voice. Photography uses a 90s flash aesthetic that carries through to the brand's Instagram.

In the app, recipes are organised on Shelves, each cook is recorded in Margins with notes and photos, and Serves handles portions. Recipes can be imported from a link or the clipboard, with AI-assisted parsing that turns cluttered web pages into clean, editable recipes. A social feed with follows, likes and notifications lets people share what they make.

The app is built natively in SwiftUI and SwiftData on a Supabase backend, with StoreKit 2 subscriptions and Sign in with Apple. It passed App Store review after several rounds, including the addition of a moderation system for user-generated content, and is live alongside a marketing site at cookwithlarder.com.
