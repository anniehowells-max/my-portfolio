If you're looking to build or improve your online presence and you've started searching for help, you've probably run into both terms: web designer and web developer. They're often used interchangeably, but they're not the same thing — and hiring the wrong one for what you actually need can cost you time and money.

Here's a clear breakdown of who does what, and how to figure out which is right for you.

### What does a web designer do?

A web designer is responsible for how a website looks, feels, and works for the person using it. This includes:

- The visual layout — structure, spacing, hierarchy
- Typography, colour, and brand consistency
- User experience (UX) — how easy the site is to navigate and use
- How the design guides a visitor toward taking an action (enquiring, booking, buying)

A good web designer thinks strategically, not just aesthetically. The goal isn't a beautiful website — it's a website that performs. Every visual decision should be in service of that.

### What does a web developer do?

A web developer takes a design and builds it in code. They're responsible for making the website actually function: the buttons work, the forms submit, the pages load quickly, and everything behaves correctly across different browsers and devices.

There are different types of developers — front-end (what you see), back-end (what happens behind the scenes), and full-stack (both). For most small business websites, front-end development is what's relevant.

> "It's less useful to ask "designer or developer?" and more useful to ask: what problem am I actually trying to solve?"

### Where it gets confusing

Some people do both. Many designers have enough development knowledge to build what they design, and many developers have a good eye for layout. Tools like Webflow and Framer have also blurred the line further, allowing designers to build fully functional sites without writing much code at all.

So when you're hiring, it's less useful to ask "designer or developer?" and more useful to ask: what problem am I actually trying to solve?

### Which one do you need?

## You probably need a designer if:

Your current site isn't converting visitors into enquiries or sales
You're not happy with how your brand is coming across online
You're starting from scratch and need someone to figure out what the site should look and feel like before it gets built
You want a site that reflects the quality of your business — not just a template with your logo dropped in

## You probably need a developer if:

You already have a clear, approved design and just need someone to build it
You need a specific technical feature (a booking system, a database, complex integrations)
Something on your existing site is broken or not working as it should

## You need both if:

You're building something from scratch with no existing design system
Your project involves complex functionality and a strong brand experience

### A practical tip

When speaking to anyone you're considering hiring, ask them to show you examples of work that solved a similar problem to yours — not just work that looks impressive. A beautiful portfolio doesn't tell you whether the site actually helped the business. A designer or developer who can talk about results as well as process is a much better signal.

### The short version

A web designer shapes the experience. A web developer builds the functionality. For most small businesses investing in their online presence, the design layer — the strategy, the clarity, the visual trust signals — is where the biggest impact tends to come from.
