The platform you build on shapes what your website can do for years.

Choose well and your site grows with you. Choose badly and you'll be paying to move everything in two years' time.

Here's how I help small businesses choose between the three most common options.

### 1. Shopify: built for selling

If selling products online is central to your business, Shopify is usually the strongest choice.

Payments, stock, shipping and discounts are built in and reliable. The app ecosystem covers almost anything else you might need.

The trade-off is cost. Monthly fees and paid apps add up, and for a service business with no shop it's often more than you need.

### 2. Squarespace: simple and polished

Squarespace suits service businesses, creatives and small brands that want a good-looking site they can update themselves.

It's easy to use and hosting is included. But templates set the limits. Custom layouts and features are difficult, and growing brands often outgrow it.

### 3. WordPress: flexible, but needs looking after

WordPress can do almost anything, which is both its strength and its risk.

With a good developer, you get a fully custom website and complete control over your content. Without one, plugins, updates and security can quickly become a burden.

> "The right platform isn't the one with the most features. It's the one that fits how your business actually works."

### How to decide

Ask yourself:

- Is selling online the main purpose of the website?
- Who will update the site day to day?
- Do you need features a template can't offer?
- Where do you expect your business to be in three years?

If you sell products, start with Shopify. If you need a polished presence you can manage yourself, Squarespace is often enough. If you need something custom and have ongoing support, WordPress or a custom build makes sense.

### Choose the platform last, not first

The most common mistake I see is picking a platform before thinking about what the website needs to do.

Good UX-led web design starts with your goals, your customers and your content. The platform should follow, not lead.
